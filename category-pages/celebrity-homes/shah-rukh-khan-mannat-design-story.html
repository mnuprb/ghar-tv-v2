Inside Mannat: The Design Story Behind Shah Rukh Khan's Iconic Bandra Residence

From a 1920s colonial bungalow to Bollywood's most iconic address — how SRK's Mannat blends heritage architecture with contemporary luxury across six floors and a sea-facing terrace.

GHAR.tv Design Studio Design Team
April 5, 2026 12 min read Updated Q2 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Mannat was originally a 1920s colonial bungalow called Villa Vienna, purchased by SRK in 2001 for ₹13.5 crore — now valued at over ₹200 crore
  • The interiors were designed by Gauri Khan, blending Art Deco influences with contemporary Indian luxury
  • The six-storey structure includes a private library, home theatre, gym, and sea-facing terrace garden
  • The property retains original colonial architectural elements — arched windows, ornate cornices, and carved wooden staircases
  • Mannat's design approach offers lessons in blending heritage conservation with modern living — applicable to any period property renovation
01

The Origin Story: From Villa Vienna to Mannat

On Bandstand promenade in Bandra West, where the Arabian Sea meets Mumbai's most storied coastline, stands a property that has become as synonymous with Bollywood as the industry itself. Mannat — the sprawling sea-facing residence of Shah Rukh Khan — draws thousands of fans to its gates every week, making it arguably the most visited private residence in India. But behind the fame and the flashbulbs lies a genuinely fascinating architectural story that spans over a century of Mumbai's built heritage.

The original structure, known as Villa Vienna, was built in the 1920s during the tail end of Bombay's colonial architectural boom. It belonged to a Gujarati business family and featured the Victorian Gothic and Art Deco elements that define Bandra's heritage bungalow belt — arched windows with stained glass panels, ornate Corinthian columns, detailed cornices with floral motifs, and high ceilings that allowed for natural ventilation in the pre-air-conditioning era. When Shah Rukh Khan purchased the property in 2001 for approximately ₹13.5 crore, he acquired not just a house but a piece of Mumbai's architectural DNA.

₹200 Cr+ estimated current market value of Mannat

The decision to rename the property Mannat — an Urdu word meaning a prayer or a wish fulfilled — was not just sentimental. It signalled an intent to transform the bungalow into something deeply personal while respecting its historical character. Over the next two decades, through multiple phases of renovation and expansion, the property would evolve from a heritage bungalow into a six-storey landmark — but always with the original colonial bones intact beneath the modern additions.

02

The Architectural Evolution

Mannat's transformation from a single-storey colonial bungalow to a six-storey residence is a case study in how to expand a heritage property without erasing its identity. The ground floor retains much of the original 1920s architecture — the arched entryway, the carved wooden staircase, and the proportions of the original rooms remain largely untouched. It is on the upper floors, added in phases between 2005 and 2019, where contemporary architecture takes over, introducing open-plan spaces, floor-to-ceiling glass panels, and terraces that maximise the property's greatest natural asset: its unobstructed Arabian Sea views.

The architectural challenge was significant: how do you add four floors to a century-old structure without compromising its foundations or its aesthetic integrity? The solution involved deep-pile foundations drilled alongside the original footings, a structural steel frame that carries the weight of the new floors independently of the old walls, and a facade treatment that gradually transitions from heritage stonework at the base to modern rendered surfaces and glass above. The visual effect, when viewed from the Bandstand promenade, is of a building that has grown organically over time rather than been abruptly expanded.

The most striking aspect of Mannat's architectural evolution is how it mirrors Mumbai's own growth story — a colonial foundation overlaid with layers of modernity, each era leaving its mark without fully erasing what came before. It is this layered quality that gives Mannat its distinctive character and distinguishes it from the sleek, ground-up luxury towers that have transformed much of Bandra's skyline.

03

Gauri Khan's Interior Vision

If the architecture of Mannat tells the story of a century-old building evolving through time, it is Gauri Khan's interior design work that gives each space its personality. As the founder of Gauri Khan Designs — a studio that has since become one of India's most prominent celebrity interior design practices — Gauri approached Mannat not as a showcase for trends but as a home that needed to work for a family with three children, a constant flow of guests from the film industry, and the occasional large-scale celebration. The result is a design language that balances opulence with livability.

The ground-floor living spaces set the tone: European classical furniture pieces — hand-sourced from auction houses in London and Paris — sit alongside custom-made Indian pieces, creating a collected-over-time aesthetic rather than a designed-all-at-once uniformity. Crystal chandeliers of varying scales hang from the original high ceilings, their formality softened by contemporary art on the walls and plush, inviting upholstery in warm neutrals. The palette throughout the formal spaces leans heavily on ivory, gold, champagne, and deep chocolate — colours that work with Mumbai's warm natural light and complement the original stonework.

  • Living rooms — European antiques mixed with custom Indian pieces; ivory and gold palette; crystal chandeliers with contemporary art
  • Master bedroom — Understated palette of soft greys and whites; full-length sea-view windows with sheer draping; bespoke headboard in Italian leather
  • Library — Floor-to-ceiling walnut shelving; vintage leather Chesterfields; focused task lighting with brass reading lamps
  • Home theatre — Acoustically engineered with tiered seating; deep burgundy velvet upholstery; a curated collection of film memorabilia
  • Terrace — Weather-resistant teak furniture; landscaped garden with indigenous coastal plants; ambient lighting for evening gatherings

04

Design Lessons from Mannat for Your Home

While few of us will ever own a sea-facing Bandra bungalow, Mannat offers design principles that translate to any scale. The most important lesson is the power of layering eras: mixing heritage elements with contemporary additions creates depth and character that no single-period design can achieve. If you live in an older apartment or bungalow in any Indian city, resist the temptation to strip everything back to a blank canvas. Instead, identify the original features worth preserving — a carved wooden door frame, original terrazzo flooring, ventilation jalis — and build your contemporary design around them.

  1. Preserve heritage bones — Original architectural details (arches, columns, terrazzo, wooden joinery) add character that no new material can replicate
  2. Layer, don't match — Mix periods and styles for a collected aesthetic that feels evolved, not designed overnight
  3. Invest in lighting — Statement fixtures in key spaces transform the mood and serve as focal points at every budget level
  4. Design for natural light — Maximise window views and use sheer draping to filter harsh Indian sunlight while maintaining openness
  5. Create functional zones — Even in smaller homes, dedicating corners for specific activities (reading, entertainment, work) adds luxury through intentionality

The second transferable lesson is about designing for your actual life, not for photographs. Mannat works as a home because it was designed around how the Khan family actually lives — spaces for children to play, for guests to gather casually, for quiet reading, and for grand celebrations. The best celebrity homes, like the best non-celebrity homes, start with an honest assessment of daily patterns and design around them, not around an Instagram aesthetic.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much did Shah Rukh Khan pay for Mannat?

Shah Rukh Khan purchased the original bungalow (then called Villa Vienna) in 2001 for approximately ₹13.5 crore. After extensive renovations and expansion over two decades, the property's estimated current market value exceeds ₹200 crore, making it one of the most valuable residential properties in Mumbai.

Who designed the interiors of Mannat?

Mannat's interiors were designed by Gauri Khan through her interior design firm Gauri Khan Designs. Gauri oversaw the complete transformation of the property, blending the original colonial architecture with contemporary luxury elements including European antiques, custom Indian furniture, and a carefully curated art collection.

How many floors does Mannat have?

Mannat is a six-storey structure that includes a basement level, a ground floor retaining much of the original 1920s colonial architecture, and four upper floors added during major renovations between 2005 and 2019. The property also features a sea-facing terrace with landscaped gardens.

What architectural style is Mannat?

Mannat is a blend of colonial-era European architecture and contemporary Indian luxury. The original 1920s structure featured Victorian Gothic and Art Deco elements — arched windows, ornate columns, and detailed cornices. Subsequent renovations introduced modern glass facades, open-plan living spaces, and a distinctive illuminated nameplate that has become a Mumbai landmark.
